Warp Bubble View

What a passenger at the centre of an Alcubierre warp bubble sees. Each pixel's light is traced back through the bubble's curved spacetime to the sky it came from, and tinted by its frequency shift. It runs on your GPU where the browser has WebGPU, and on the CPU where it does not.

What you are looking at

The sky is a reference pattern: a checkerboard in longitude and latitude, warm ahead of the ship and cool behind, with a line every 30°. Its distortion is the bubble's lensing and nothing else. At zero speed you see it undistorted.

The tint is the light's frequency shift, blue where it arrives with more energy than it left with and red where less. That shift is exact: the metric depends on time and on the direction of travel only through x − v t, so p_t + v p_x is conserved along every ray. It follows that light arrives shifted by exactly 1 − v cosα, whatever the bubble's speed. Light from straight ahead is blueshifted by 1 + v.

Above the speed of light, black appears behind the ship. No ray from the sky reaches the passenger from those directions. Traced backwards, their light piles up at the bubble's rear horizon with its energy growing without bound.

The physics is in raytracer.js. The test suite loads that file in Node and compares its rays, ray by ray, with ParticleSim's Python tracer (particlesim.analysis.raytrace). The Python tracer is itself checked against an independent integrator of the geodesic equation to 1e-10. The GPU shader repeats the same steps in single precision.
